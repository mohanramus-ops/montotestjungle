MFJ Tier-1 Bird Content Blueprint — 50 Topics

Read this first: These are pattern-matched against your proven format ("do birds have X" / "why do birds X"), all globally-relevant with no India-specific skew (avoids the RPM trap we identified with the koel idea). I do NOT have live keyword-volume data for most of these — only the ~8 I actually searched today. Volume estimates below are honest confidence levels, not real numbers. Before writing any of these, paste me the topic and I'll do a real competition check the way we did for Kea, UV vision, knees, etc. — don't skip that step, since roughly half of today's "obvious" guesses turned out to be saturated once checked.

Confidence key:

  • ✅ VERIFIED TODAY — actually checked, real data behind the note
  • 🟡 HIGH CONFIDENCE — strong pattern match to proven format, not yet checked
  • ⚪ WORTH CHECKING — plausible but genuinely unknown, check before committing

A. Body & Anatomy (extends your proven "Do Birds Have X" series)

  1. Do birds have kneecaps? 🟡 — sets up the backwards-knees myth piece naturally
  2. Do birds have collarbones? 🟡 — the furcula/wishbone is genuinely interesting and underexplained (it's a shock absorber for flight, not just a wishing bone)
  3. Do birds have ribs? ⚪
  4. Do birds have fingers? 🟡 — wing bones = modified fingers, ties directly to your Wings article
  5. Do birds have blood the same color as ours? ⚪ — simple, visual, shareable
  6. Do birds have a voice box like humans? 🟡 — standalone syrinx piece, different framing than your Lips article's syrinx section
  7. Do birds have muscles like ours? ⚪ — flight-muscle percentage of body weight is a wild stat worth checking
  8. Do birds have kneecaps that show? — SKIP (duplicate of #1)
  9. Do birds have skin under their feathers? ⚪
  10. Do birds have belly fat / can they get obese? ⚪ — genuinely underexplored angle

B. Senses & Perception (beyond what Noses/Eyelids/Tongues already cover)

  1. Can birds hear better than humans? 🟡
  2. Can owls really see in total darkness? 🟡 — good myth-correction angle (they can't see in zero light, contrary to popular belief)
  3. Do birds have a good memory? 🟡 — Clark's nutcracker caching thousands of seeds is a great "wow" stat
  4. Can birds recognize individual human faces? ✅ HIGH CONFIDENCE — crow facial-recognition research is well-documented, genuinely viral-worthy, likely global search behavior
  5. Do birds feel pain? ⚪ — sensitive but legitimate, high genuine curiosity
  6. Do birds get dizzy? ⚪
  7. Can birds taste spicy food? — SKIP (already covered in your Tongues article via parakeet/capsaicin)
  8. Do birds have a sense of time? ⚪ — circadian rhythm angle, ties to your Sleep article
  9. Can birds see behind themselves without turning their heads? 🟡 — genuinely wild fact for prey species with eyes on the sides of their heads
  10. Do baby birds recognize their parents by sight or sound? ⚪

C. Physiology & Survival

  1. Do birds get cold? 🟡 — ties to your Feathers insulation content, good expansion angle
  2. Do birds sweat? ⚪ — quick, punchy, shareable
  3. How fast does a hummingbird's heart actually beat? 🟡 — concrete numbers make for a great snippet/shareable stat
  4. Can birds get sunburned? ⚪
  5. Do birds get goosebumps? ⚪ — genuinely interesting since they don't have the same skin structure
  6. Can all birds swim, or just water birds? ⚪
  7. Do birds catch colds the way we do? ⚪
  8. Can a bird survive a lightning strike? ⚪
  9. Do birds dream? 🟡 — REM sleep research in birds is real and interesting, ties directly to your Sleep article
  10. How do birds survive winter without migrating? 🟡 — torpor already touched in Sleep article; this could be the fuller standalone piece

D. Myths & Misconceptions (your strongest angle — confused SERPs are winnable)

  1. Will a mother bird abandon her chicks if you touch them? ✅ HIGH CONFIDENCE — this is one of the most widely believed FALSE myths in the English-speaking world, near-certain high volume, near-certain a "correct the record" piece can win on authority + clarity
  2. Can owls really turn their heads all the way around? 🟡 — classic myth (they can't do a full 360°, but they get remarkably close — the real mechanism is fascinating)
  3. Do vultures circle over dying animals? 🟡 — pop-culture trope, real answer is more nuanced (they mostly find food by smell/sight after death, not by circling the dying)
  4. Do ostriches really bury their heads in the sand? 🟡 — one of the most famous false animal myths in the language, must-have candidate
  5. Do all birds fly south for the winter? ⚪ — nuance opportunity (many don't migrate at all)
  6. Are penguins the only flightless birds? ⚪
  7. Do robins only eat worms? ⚪
  8. Can eagles really carry off small dogs or children? 🟡 — persistent viral myth, satisfying to correctly debunk with real prey-weight data
  9. Do birds abandon their nests if a predator's scent is on it? ⚪
  10. Is it true birds have no sense of smell? — SKIP (already covered in your Noses article)

E. Records, Extremes & Comparisons

  1. What is the fastest bird in the world? 🟡 — peregrine falcon dive speed, very high natural search interest, but check competition (likely to be contested)
  2. What bird has the largest wingspan alive today? 🟡 — wandering albatross, strong evergreen interest
  3. What is the smallest bird in the world? 🟡 — bee hummingbird, classic but evergreen
  4. Crow vs. raven: what's actually different? 🟡 — extremely common confusion, good comparison-format piece, likely real competition but a clean, well-sourced comparison table can win
  5. Hawk vs. falcon vs. eagle: how to tell them apart? ⚪ — practical, evergreen, birdwatcher-intent traffic (often good RPM since birdwatching audiences skew affluent hobbyist)
  6. Owl vs. hawk: key differences? ⚪
  7. Which bird lays the largest egg relative to its body size? 🟡 — kiwi egg fact is genuinely wild (up to 20% of the female's body weight)
  8. Why are flamingos pink? ⚪ — classic evergreen question, MUST check competition before committing, likely saturated given how iconic the fact is
  9. Why do some birds sleep standing on one leg? 🟡 — direct expansion of your existing Sleep article, good internal-link opportunity
  10. Why do some birds lay blue or speckled eggs? ⚪ — genuinely under-explained mechanism (biliverdin pigment) in most consumer content

How to use this blueprint

  1. Pick 3-5 candidates per session, not all 50 blind — paste each one to me and I'll run the same verification we did for Kea/UV vision/Knees before you commit writing time.
  2. Prioritize the ✅ and 🟡 items first — they have the strongest pattern-match to what's already worked for you today.
  3. Items marked SKIP are already covered by your live articles — don't duplicate.
  4. Treat "myths" (Section D) as your highest-probability category overall: false beliefs generate consistently high, geography-neutral search volume, and a correctly-sourced debunk is one of the most reliable ways to outrank confused or shallow competitors — which is exactly the pattern that worked for your Kea and ostrich-rudder facts.
